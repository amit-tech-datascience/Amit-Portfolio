Master’s thesis · 2025

Learning with physical constraints

Investigating how training choices affect neural solutions to Helmholtz and Allen–Cahn equations.

StatusM.Sc. thesis · completed June 2025

Tools & methodsTensorFlow, PINNs, PDEs, Hydra

EvidenceExplore the thesis repository ↗

The problem

Physics-informed neural networks combine boundary conditions with differential-equation residuals. Their training behavior depends on how those constraints are represented and optimized.

Conceptual illustration, not an experimental output.

My contribution

For my master’s work, I implemented and committed PINN experiment scripts and saved solution, residual and error outputs, using TensorDiffEq and pinns-tf2 as existing frameworks.

The approach

The experiments explore Helmholtz and Allen–Cahn problems, collocation strategies, adaptive weighting and optimizer choices. The repository contains configurations, training logs and solution visualizations.

Results & learning

The work produced an experiment collection for examining neural PDE solutions and their residuals. It connects deep learning with numerical methods and scientific computing.

Explore the saved experiments

The repository includes actual solution, residual and absolute-error figures from my Allen–Cahn experiments. These links point to the same archived commit so the evidence stays traceable.

Associated experiment script: 50,000 collocation points, four 128-unit hidden layers, adaptive weights and periodic boundary conditions. No execution manifest ties the saved pixels to a specific run; these figures do not establish a final model ranking.

What this establishes

I do not claim authorship of the underlying PINN frameworks. A consistent final comparison has not been established for this portfolio, so no numerical improvement is presented.
